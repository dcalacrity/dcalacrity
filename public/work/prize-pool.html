A new kind of film

What is an Open World Movie?

Walk anywhere in the story. Choose by what you do. The story still lands.

Prize Pool VR is a live-action 6DoF movie built around real performances. Most of the film is 360° live-action cinema: real actors filmed on green screen and composited into the virtual reality space. These scenes use three degrees of freedom (3DoF): you can look all around, while your viewing position stays fixed.

At specific choice sequences, the experience opens into six degrees of freedom (6DoF): you can move through the scene and choose through what you do and where you go. Only these sequences use actor-scanned Unreal Engine MetaHumans, creating hyperrealistic 3D versions of the cast within environments captured from real locations. The 360° scenes and walkable choices form one cinematic story, and the story still lands.

As far as we know, Prize Pool VR is the first film built this way. Our aim is to break barriers for 360° live-action VR cinematic experiences by bringing embodied, walkable choices into a movie led by real actors.

The premise

Get wet, you’re out. Stay dry, win a million.

It’s the seventh annual Campus Assassin at Adam’s University: a water-gun game across campus. This year an anonymous donation turns a forty-thousand-dollar pot into one million dollars. Teams are allowed. Only one dry player takes the prize.

The rules are simple. Get wet, or step outside the boundary that shrinks as players go out, and you’re done. Every shot puts you on everyone’s map for thirty seconds. Hidden security cameras confirm every hit, even when nobody else is looking.

Someone put forty of those cameras around campus. Nobody seems to know who, or why they want to watch. Then two strangers collide in the square just before the horn, and you choose whose day to live.

Play as

Hope

Everyone has decided who she is.

Hope is the glue of her friend group: the one who laughs it off, smooths it over and keeps quiet when it would cost her. Her boyfriend, Simon, has already planned the route, divided the shares and given her a role. Her friends love her, mostly in ways that keep her exactly where she is.

Nobody has asked what Hope wants. There’s a studio viewing on her phone, booked for one.

Stay small for the people who need you to, or find out who you are without them. Hope can only break out if you do.

Play as

Chase

Two faces. One prize.

Chase has a grin for everyone, and a different one when he thinks nobody is looking. A stranger calls him two-face within a minute of meeting him, and isn’t wrong. His best friends have split the winnings three ways and sworn to have his back. Chase swore too.

A million dollars is a lot of reasons to break a promise.

Chase will do anything to win, if you let him. Which face he shows is your call, and so is what it costs.

Same game. Same day. Two very different people, and who they become is up to you.

Character names may change before release.

How it’s made

Real places. Real performances. Room to walk.

A live-action 6DoF movie built in four steps: map the locations, capture the world, film the actors and open the choices to movement.

01 · Plan

Map the story

Google Maps and Google Earth Projects help storyboard locations, movement and camera viewpoints before the shoot. On-site planning connects the story to the real place.

02 · Capture

Build from real places

We’re refining a measured Gaussian Splatting process to capture Wilmington locations, check scale against on-site measurements and bring them into Unreal Engine. The capture workflow is in development.

03 · Perform

Real actors. A 360° world.

Most of the movie features real actors filmed on green screen and composited into the VR space, with spatial audio. These 3DoF scenes let you look in every direction from a fixed viewing position.

04 · Choose

Step into the decision

Specific choices open into 6DoF movement. Only these sequences use actor-scanned Unreal Engine MetaHumans: hyperrealistic 3D versions of the cast in captured real-world environments, our closest approach to live-action 6DoF.
