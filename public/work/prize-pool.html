A new kind of film

What is an Open World Movie?

Walk anywhere in the story. Choose by what you do. The story still lands.

An Open World Movie is a narrative film you walk through. You are bodily present in the scene with six degrees of freedom (6DoF), so you can move freely instead of watching from a fixed camera position. You make your choices through what you do and where you go, not through menus.

Wherever you stand, the story always lands: the drama still plays, whatever path you take. And the characters are real actors’ performances, not animated stand-ins.

The premise

Get wet, you’re out. Stay dry, win a million.

It’s the seventh annual Campus Assassin at Adam’s University: a water-gun game across campus. This year an anonymous donation turns a forty-thousand-dollar pot into one million dollars. Teams are allowed. Only one dry player takes the prize.

The rules are simple. Get wet, or step outside the boundary that shrinks as players go out, and you’re done. Every shot puts you on everyone’s map for thirty seconds. Hidden security cameras confirm every hit, even when nobody else is looking.

Someone put forty of those cameras around campus. Nobody seems to know who, or why they want to watch. Then two strangers collide in the square just before the horn, and you choose whose day to live.

How it’s made

Real places. Real performances. Room to walk.

Real Wilmington, North Carolina locations, the cast’s own performances, 360° live-action scenes and spatial audio, brought together into one world you can move through.

Image coming soon · captured location

Real places you can walk through

Real Wilmington locations are captured as explorable 3D spaces with Gaussian splatting, then rebuilt in Unreal Engine.

Image coming soon · actor and digital double

Real performances, digital doubles

Actors perform on a 210° green-screen stage, and their own performances drive Unreal Engine MetaHuman digital doubles you can walk around.

Image coming soon · 360° window scene

Windows into memory

360° live-action “window” scenes open into the story, with spatial audio that places every sound where it happens.
