The pitch

Uber met the Silk Road. Fifteen years later, they run the world.

Picture a freelance marketplace with one rule: there are no rules. You can rescue a lost pet on Tuesday and help pull off an international incident on Friday. You’re paid in untraceable K.U.M. You earn XP. Your five-star rating opens more doors than your diploma ever did. It looks like a video game. It runs like the shadow economy that quietly replaced the real one.

Now meet its newest player. Adam Dawnbringer is twenty, a college kid and grocery clerk making $589 a week, with a hero complex, a pickup truck he’s embarrassed by, and a date that died the moment she saw it. He wants a $50,000 supercar in three weeks. His mother wants him to earn honest money. His friends want him to say yes to Sidequest. And his father, a military veteran who went missing chasing dirty money, left him one inheritance: life is like a video game.

So Adam does what any reluctant hero does. He gets the band back together.

When the legal economy offers scraps, an illegal game becomes the most rational career path.

The tone: Mr. Robot’s paranoia × Barry’s heart × Baby Driver’s needle drops × GTA Online’s interface. Live-action series with first-person moments and a hand-built game HUD.

Two eras

Version 0.1 to version 4.0.

The story spans fifteen years of one platform’s history: from an invite-only tool for criminals to the job market everyone uses and nobody admits to.

Fifteen years ago · the prologue

A black-market terminal and a backseat offer.

Before the holograms and the leaderboards, Sidequest was a raw text terminal with two options, START GAME and EXIT, known only to fixers and smugglers. Cain Dawnbringer is a veteran driving rideshare for tips, raising a five-year-old son and losing a slow war against his bills.

One night an old war buddy slides into his backseat.

“It’s a cold world, Cain. You gotta cheat the system if you want to win. How about a Sidequest?”

Today · Season 1 Part 1

Mainstream culture. Still illegal.

Sidequest now has holographic Quest Brokers, a Mystery Box job randomizer, co-op missions, zero-trace payouts and a HUD over everything. Radio hosts argue about people calling Sidequest instead of the police. Gen Z treats a five-star rating like a résumé.

The son is twenty now. He has never been told what that first job cost his family. That is where the story begins.

How the game works

A $15 trillion economy needs rules.

A market that size can’t be a lawless void. It has currency, ranking, dispatch and enforcement. Somebody writes the code. As the in-world broadcast puts it, no one knows who’s really in control.

The hypocrisy

Everybody uses it. Nobody admits it.

That is why Sidequest has survived fifteen years of federal crackdowns. Nobody in this world is recruited by a supervillain. They’re recruited by a bad month.

The case file

The game has an owner.

Sidequest runs inside the Cyber Realm, the most encrypted network ever built. Federal agencies call it the Second Silk Road. Its creation myth begins in Nigeria, with a builder the world came to know as the Trojan Stallion. Officially, he’s dead.

  1. His house exploded in the largest raid in FBI history. So why did his account just log back in?
  2. Who writes the rules, holds the escrow and runs the brokers of a $15 trillion illegal economy?
  3. What happened to Cain Dawnbringer after he pressed START GAME, and where is he now?
  4. Why does a motivational guru keep all of his research on paper?
  5. Which member of the squad is keeping the biggest secret?

Literary inspirations

The books underneath it.

On the surface, Sidequest is a heist comedy with a game interface. Underneath, it’s in conversation with some very old stories about fathers, brothers, gifts that aren’t gifts, and what poverty makes reasonable.

The Odyssey

Homer

The epic opens with a son, Telemachus, setting out to find a father who never came home.

Adam says he wants a supercar. What he’s really on is the oldest quest there is.

Genesis 4

The story of Cain and Abel

The first question after the first crime: “Am I my brother’s keeper?”

War buddies, a crew of old friends, a twin. The name Cain was not chosen by accident.

The Aeneid, Book II

Virgil

Troy doesn’t fall to a siege. It falls to a gift it wheels in through its own gates.

The Trojan Stallion never breaks in. It starts with an email, then a small charge.

Things Fall Apart

Chinua Achebe

Tradition, ambition and a new economic order colliding in southeastern Nigeria.

The Cyber Realm’s creation myth begins in Nigeria. So do the writer’s own roots.

Oliver Twist

Charles Dickens

Fagin doesn’t need to threaten anyone. Hunger does his recruiting for him.

Nelson’s warehouse is the same gate, fifteen years before the world catches on.

Crime and Punishment

Fyodor Dostoevsky

A broke student convinces himself that one crime is the rational choice, then has to live with it.

Every Sidequest job is a small Raskolnikov argument, with XP attached.

Neuromancer & Snow Crash

William Gibson · Neal Stephenson

Cyberspace as a place, and a gig economy run like a game, decades before either existed.

The Cyber Realm is what happens when that future arrives as an app.

American Kingpin

Nick Bilton

The true story of the Silk Road, and of the federal agents convicted of stealing from the market they were sent to shut down.

Team 4B’s temptation is drawn from the real record.

“Social Structure and Anomie”

Robert K. Merton, 1938

Strain theory: when society blocks the legitimate road to success, people invent other roads.

The show’s whole thesis, in one essay. Sidequest is the other road.

One world, many screens

Built to run for years.

Sidequest was written as a world, not a one-off. The show bible covers two eras, fourteen fully profiled characters, the rules of an entire economy, and season mysteries with answers already written. That gives one property room for many formats.

How it’s made

Cinema with a game’s nervous system.

Mostly third-person cinema, cut with first-person moments: waking HUD boot-ups, fight flashes and character switches. Spatial audio is recorded on set with an ambisonic array and a binaural rig. The HUD is designed by hand in Blender and After Effects. The look pairs punchy 1980s contrast with vivid 2000s colour. The sound is “nostalgia of a new age”: 2000s rock, orchestra, pop-punk, electronic jazz and rap.

The production history

Three phases, from Raleigh to Wilmington.

Sidequest began before Pure Alacrity existed. About 28 shooting days in total, across the beta, the main production and pickups.

1 · November–December 2024

Raleigh, community college. The early Episode 1 beta.

2 · 2025

May, Wilmington: the commercial scene. June–August, Raleigh: most of Episodes 2–3 and parts of Episode 1. November, Wilmington: the prologue.

3 · January–October 2026

Wilmington pickups. Additional photography.

Where Pure Alacrity came from. Pure development began in January 2026, after most of the footage for Episodes 1–3 had been shot. Making the series by hand, with every change rippling through schedules, budgets and call sheets, is what led D.C Agwu to build his own production software. Pure did not plan or run the original 2024–2025 shoots.
